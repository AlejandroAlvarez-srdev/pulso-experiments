01 · EDA and data cleaning¶

Simulated data. Every row in this notebook comes from pulso_exp.simulate with a fixed seed. Pulso is a fictional company; nothing here describes real merchants, customers or results.

Goal: understand the four tables the experiment and the ETA model are built on, check data quality, and make every cleaning decision explicit (what, how many rows, why) before any analysis touches the data.

The simulator deliberately plants problems a real pipeline would have: duplicated ingestion rows, bot/integration-test merchants, amounts entered ×100, deliveries stuck for hours, and missing values. A good EDA should find all of them without peeking at the generator.

2 · Data quality checks¶

Generic checks first — key uniqueness, null rates, value ranges — then targeted looks at whatever they flag.

def quality_summary(df: pd.DataFrame, key: str) -> pd.Series:
    nulls = df.isna().mean()
    return pd.Series(
        {
            "rows": len(df),
            "duplicate keys": int(df[key].duplicated().sum()),
            "fully duplicated rows": int(df.duplicated().sum()),
            "columns with nulls": ", ".join(f"{c} ({v:.1%})" for c, v in nulls[nulls > 0].items())
            or "-",
        }
    )

).T

Findings so far

  • sessions has duplicated session_ids and they are full-row duplicates → an ingestion retry, not two sessions sharing an id. Safe to drop.
  • device and page_load_ms have a few percent missing; pulser_rating is missing on some orders.
  • Nothing is out of physical range, but there is a long delivery tail (> 4 h) and some client timeouts.
  • Referential integrity holds: every order comes from a paid session.

A clean gap separates ordinary merchants (the heaviest real merchants stay below 100 sessions a period) from a cluster with hundreds of sessions and zero paid orders, all on web. That is integration/bot traffic. Because it is assigned to variants like any merchant, leaving it in would dilute conversion and inflate session counts in whichever arm it lands.

Rule: a merchant with ≥ 100 checkout sessions in a period and no paid order is excluded from every analysis. The rule is defined on behaviour that the treatment cannot cause (a bot does not convert in either arm).

The main mass is a log-normal around a few hundred MXN; a separate bump sits exactly ~100× higher, with the same per-category shape. That is the signature of an amount typed in cents into a pesos field (or vice versa), not of genuinely large baskets — a real large-basket tail would be smooth.

Rule: amounts above 50× the category median are divided by 100 and flagged (amount_fixed). Risk: a genuine 50× basket would be wrongly shrunk. At this threshold, the clean log-normal places essentially no mass there, so the expected false-fix count is ~0; the flag keeps the decision auditable.

Past ~2.5 h the distribution stops decaying and turns into a flat plateau: those are orders that got stuck (pulser phone dead, order not closed in the app). The 4 h cut is deliberately conservative — a few stuck orders sit between 2.5 h and 4 h and stay in, which costs a little model accuracy but never removes a genuinely slow delivery. They are real operational events — keep them for ops metrics — but they are not information about how long a delivery takes, so they are excluded when training the ETA model (notebook 03).

What is not done, on purpose:

  • No imputation of page_load_ms. Missingness is unrelated to arm here (checked below); the latency guardrail is a percentile, so dropping NaNs is honest while imputing a median would shrink the tail we care about.
  • Timeouts are kept. A 30 s page load is the worst user experience there is; removing it would flatter the p95.
  • pulser_rating stays NaN. Missing means "new pulser", which is itself informative; gradient boosting handles it natively.

Distance dominates, but its slope depends on the vehicle: bikes keep up on trips under ~2 km and fall far behind beyond 4 km, while motos are fastest at every distance. That vehicle × distance interaction is something a tree model picks up for free and the legacy 10 + 4·km quote ignores.

5 · Takeaways¶

  1. Five data problems found and handled: duplicate sessions (dropped), 15 bot merchants (excluded everywhere), ×100 amount typos (fixed + flagged), stuck deliveries > 4 h (flagged, excluded from model training), and missing device/latency/rating values (kept, each with a stated reason).
  2. The funnel leaks most at details → payment, the step the one-page checkout targets.
  3. Arms are balanced before the test, so differences during the experiment can be attributed to the treatment.

The cleaned tables are rebuilt the same way at the top of notebooks 02 and 03 (clean_sessions / clean_orders), so there is a single definition of "clean".
